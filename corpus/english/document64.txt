TITLE: Languages, Multilingualism and Literature of India

India is one of the most linguistically diverse nations on Earth, home to hundreds of living languages representing Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, Austroasiatic, and Tibeto-Burman language families. The Constitution of India recognizes twenty-two major languages, including Hindi, Telugu, Tamil, Bengali, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi, and Sanskrit. Multilingualism is an integral facet of daily life, cultural communication, and national identity.

Indian literature encompasses a vast treasury of ancient poetry, classical epics, philosophical texts, dramas, and modern novels. From ancient Sanskrit epics and Tamil Sangam poetry to medieval Bhakti devotional literature and contemporary vernacular prose, writers have expressed profound human emotions and social commentary. In university language and literature departments, students and scholars analyze literary history, translation studies, and comparative literature.

In the digital era, computational linguistics and natural language processing are opening new horizons for Indian languages. Researchers develop Unicode fonts, machine translation models, speech recognition tools, and digital keyboards for Indian scripts. Promoting regional language literature and modern digital linguistic tools democratizes education, enhances public communication, and strengthens linguistic culture across India.
